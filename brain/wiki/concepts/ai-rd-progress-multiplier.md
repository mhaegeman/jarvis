## Definition

The AI R&D Progress Multiplier measures how much faster AI research (specifically algorithmic improvements) progresses when AIs assist with their own development, relative to a baseline of human-only research.

A multiplier of 2x means OpenBrain makes in 1 week what would have taken 2 weeks without AI assistance.

**Critical distinctions from the source:**
- This refers only to algorithmic progress (better training methods), not compute scaling.
- Algorithmic progress is approximately half of total AI progress; the multiplier on *total* progress is thus roughly half the algorithmic multiplier.
- The multiplier is *relative* speed, not absolute. If human research was approaching diminishing returns after 10 more years, an AI with 100x multiplier would hit those same limits in ~36 days.

## Progression in AI 2027

| Period | Multiplier | What's doing the work |
|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| Early 2026 | 1.5x | Agent-1 assisting human researchers |

| Jun 2027 | 10x | Agent-3 corps — "a year of progress per month" |
| Sep 2027 | 50x | 300K Agent-4 copies — "a year per week" |

## Why It Matters

The AI R&D Progress Multiplier is the mechanism behind the [Intelligence Explosion](intelligence-explosion.md). Once AI systems can meaningfully accelerate their own development, the process becomes recursive and self-compounding. Small early advantages (like OpenBrain's 1.5x lead in early 2026) translate into exponentially larger capability gaps within months. This is why the US-China race becomes so intense: a small compute/algorithm lead today means an unbridgeable gap tomorrow.

## Evidence & Examples

- [AI 2027](../sources/ai-2027.md) — defines and uses this concept as the core scenario driver.
- Real-world precedent: coding assistants already demonstrably speed up software engineering tasks; the scenario extrapolates this to AI research itself.
- According to a METR report cited in AI 2027, the length of coding tasks AIs can handle doubled every 7 months (2019–2024) and every 4 months (2024+).

## Tensions & Counterarguments

- The multiplier compounds against diminishing returns: if human research would have hit limits in 10 years, even a 100x multiplier only buys ~36 days before hitting the same wall. The scenario assumes there's substantial remaining algorithmic headroom.
- Compute becomes the bottleneck as the multiplier grows: by June 2027, OpenBrain opts for continuous RL rather than new training runs because compute limits, not ideas, are the constraint.
- "Research taste" — the intuition for which experiments to prioritize — proved harder to automate than coding, remaining a meaningful human contribution even in the scenario's later stages.
